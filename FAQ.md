# FAQ.md — Frequently Asked Questions (TCT)
## Reviewer-Oriented Clarifications (v1.0)

This document addresses the most likely questions, objections,
and misunderstandings regarding the Theory of Coherent Time (TCT).

---

## Q1: Is TCT a reformulation of General Relativity?

**Short answer:** Possibly — and that is acceptable.

**Detailed answer:**  
TCT does not claim to replace General Relativity.
It proposes an alternative underlying description in which
GR emerges as an effective geometric limit of stationary coherence configurations.

If TCT reduces exactly to GR with no new experimentally distinguishable effects,
then TCT is redundant and should be rejected.
This is explicitly acknowledged.

---

## Q2: What is new in TCT compared to existing physics?

TCT introduces:
- a scalar coherence field 𝓒(x,t),
- a concrete, falsifiable prediction of correlated temporal noise
  between independent precision clocks.

The theory stands or falls on this prediction alone.

---

## Q3: Is coherence just entropy, information, or quantum entanglement?

No.

𝓒 is defined as an independent physical quantity.
While correlations with entropy, information, or entanglement may exist,
no reduction is assumed or required.

Any such reduction must be demonstrated, not postulated.

---

## Q4: Does TCT violate causality or introduce retrocausation?

No.

All dynamics of 𝓒 are local and causal.
No signal, information, or influence propagates faster than light.
Observed correlations are statistical and non-signaling.

---

## Q5: Does TCT predict observable effects beyond current experiments?

No.

TCT v1.0 predicts a single effect:
low-frequency correlated timing noise.

All other implications (cosmology, phases, cognition) are explicitly excluded
from the core model and treated as speculative extensions.

---

## Q6: Why hasn’t this effect been observed already?

Possible reasons include:
- focus on single-clock noise rather than cross-correlation,
- insufficient long-term integration,
- masking by known low-frequency systematics.

Existing datasets may already constrain or falsify TCT,
but have not been analyzed in the required correlation-focused manner.

---

## Q7: What would falsify TCT conclusively?

A null result:
- no detectable correlation between independent clocks,
- at sensitivity below the predicted coherence-induced level,
- across the relevant low-frequency band.

In that case, TCT v1.0 is rejected without modification.

---

## Q8: Can TCT be tuned to evade falsification?

No.

The core prediction and falsification criterion are fixed in EXPERIMENT.md.
Parameter tuning beyond stated bounds is disallowed by design.

---

## Q9: Is TCT a theory of consciousness, free will, or metaphysics?

No.

Such interpretations are explicitly outside the scope of the core theory.
They may be explored separately but do not affect the validity of TCT.

---

## Q10: Why introduce a new field instead of modifying existing ones?

Because the predicted effect is not naturally described
by metric curvature, standard quantum noise, or known interactions.

If future work shows that 𝓒 is reducible to existing fields,
TCT collapses into a reformulation and loses independent status.

---

## Q11: What is the minimum experiment required?

Two or more independent precision clocks,
with long-term cross-correlation analysis after standard corrections.

No exotic technology is required.

---

## Q12: What is the current status of TCT?

TCT is a **hypothesis with a single falsifiable prediction**.
It is neither confirmed nor accepted.
Its value depends entirely on experimental outcome.

---

## Final Note

TCT does not ask for belief.
It asks for a specific analysis of existing or future data.

If the predicted correlation is absent, the theory ends here.
