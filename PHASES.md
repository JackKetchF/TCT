# PHASES.md — Regimes of the Coherence Field (TCT)
## Canonical Phase Structure (v1.0)

This document defines the **phase structure** of Coherent Time Theory (TCT).
Phases are **not additional postulates**; they are **regimes of solutions** of the coherence field equation defined in MODEL.md.

---

## Definition

A *phase* is defined as a dynamically stable or metastable regime of the coherence field 𝓒(x,t),
characterized by the relative dominance of:
- temporal gradients ∂ₜ𝓒,
- spatial gradients ∇𝓒,
- nonlinear self-interaction dV/d𝓒,
- coupling to external fields via the metric g_{μν}.

Phases are universal and scale-invariant: the same phase structure applies from microscopic to cosmological systems.

---

## Phase I — Potentiality

**Regime**
- |∇𝓒| ≈ 0
- |∂ₜ𝓒| ≈ 0
- V(𝓒) locally flat

**Description**
The system occupies a high-degeneracy coherence configuration.
No preferred temporal ordering is selected.
Multiple future evolutions are dynamically allowed.

**Physical Interpretation**
- Quantum superposition regime
- High uncertainty, low constraint
- Pre-selection state

---

## Phase II — Differentiation

**Regime**
- Small but growing |∇𝓒|
- ∂ₜ𝓒 begins to deviate from zero

**Description**
Local variations of coherence emerge.
Symmetry between possible evolutions begins to break.

**Physical Interpretation**
- Initial decoherence
- Emergence of distinguishable alternatives
- Context-dependent constraints appear

---

## Phase III — Coherence

**Regime**
- Strong spatial correlation of 𝓒
- |∇𝓒| minimized across subsystems
- Phase-locked evolution of ∂ₜ𝓒

**Description**
Multiple subsystems share a common coherence configuration.
Temporal evolution becomes mutually consistent.

**Physical Interpretation**
- Entanglement / synchronization
- Stable structures
- Long-range correlations without signaling

---

## Phase IV — Realization

**Regime**
- Rapid increase in |∂ₜ𝓒|
- One branch dynamically dominates

**Description**
A specific temporal trajectory is selected.
Alternative evolutions become dynamically inaccessible.

**Physical Interpretation**
- Measurement-like transition
- Classical outcome emergence
- Event actualization

---

## Phase V — Stabilization

**Regime**
- ∂ₜ𝓒 → constant
- |∇𝓒| small but nonzero

**Description**
The realized configuration persists.
The system resists small perturbations.

**Physical Interpretation**
- Classical persistence
- Memory formation
- Structural stability

---

## Phase VI — Dissipation

**Regime**
- Gradual decay of |∇𝓒|
- Energy leakage into environment
- Increasing entropy in coupled degrees of freedom

**Description**
Coherence degrades due to interaction with surroundings.
Temporal correlations weaken.

**Physical Interpretation**
- Decoherence
- Thermalization
- Aging of structures

---

## Phase VII — Reset

**Regime**
- 𝓒 approaches background level
- Loss of previous phase correlations

**Description**
The system returns to a neutral coherence state.
Conditions for renewed potentiality are restored.

**Physical Interpretation**
- End of cycle
- Preparation for re-entry into Phase I
- No memory at the level of 𝓒 alone

---

## Universality

The same phase structure applies to:
- atoms (quantum transitions),
- molecules (bond formation/breaking),
- biological systems (metabolism, cognition),
- societies (coordination, crisis),
- cosmology (structure formation).

Differences arise only in scale, coupling strength, and boundary conditions.
